# Stage A Landmark Prompts (Gemini)

**How to use:** generate one image per prompt. Paste the **Style Header** first, then the landmark prompt, as one message. Generate 2-4 variations each and keep the best. Save to `docs/reference/landmarks/<name>.png` (reference only, never shipped).

**Consistency tips**
- Use the exact same Style Header every time.
- Ask for landscape 16:9.
- If a result drifts in style, attach your best previous landmark image and add: "Match the art style, lighting, and camera angle of the attached image exactly."
- If text or logos appear, regenerate; don't edit them out.

---

## Style Header (paste before every prompt)

> Epic high-fantasy strategy game campaign map illustration. Painterly, richly detailed digital painting with saturated but natural colors, heroic exaggerated scale, and grim, weathered detail, in the spirit of modern fantasy grand-strategy campaign maps. Camera: high three-quarter aerial view, about 50 degrees down, looking at the landmark from slightly above and to one side, the way a player sees it on a campaign map. The landmark is shown at its grandest, fully developed stage and fills the center of the frame, with its surrounding countryside visible around it. Lighting: clear late-morning daylight from the upper left, soft shadows, minimal fog, so every building shape, wall, and roof reads clearly (this image is a reference for building a 3D model). The world feels alive through tiny, simple details at this scale: specks of people on roads and walls, tiny carts, plumes of chimney smoke, banners, small boats, birds suggested by a couple of brush strokes. Surrounding the landmark: its countryside, roads converging on it, smaller villages and hamlets outside the walls, fields, and terrain that shows how the land around it is used. Original design only: no text, no labels, no UI, no logos, no real-world religious symbols, no symbols from existing games or franchises. Landscape 16:9.

## 1. Caeloth, the Throne City (holy city at the heart of the continent)

> Caeloth, the holy Throne City, the largest and richest city in the world, built where three great rivers meet in a vast marshland. The city is enormous and overwhelming in scale, spreading across several islands joined by monumental stone bridges, ringed by multiple concentric walls of pale gold-white stone with massive gatehouses. At its heart rises the Cathedral of the Undying Throne: a colossal domed basilica of white marble and gold, far taller than anything else, its great dome crowned with a radiant golden spire, flanked by soaring towers and flying buttresses, a golden glow spilling from tall windows. Around it: dense districts of tiled roofs, market islands crowded with stalls, pilgrim plazas, smaller temples, palaces of rich merchants, and harbors full of river barges. The marsh-moats are beautiful: clear canals, water gardens, reed beds, and arched bridges, not grim swamp. Pilgrim roads lined with shrines converge on the city from every direction, crowded with tiny travelers and caravans. Outside the walls, sprawling suburbs, villages, farmland, and river docks. It should feel sacred, wealthy, ancient, and awe-inspiring, the jewel of the world.

## 2. Crownhaven (Roman capital, seat of the Senate)

> Crownhaven, the ancient imperial capital of a fantasy Roman civilization, built across three hills above a river mouth on a warm coast. Strongly Roman in character, with a heroic fantasy twist in scale. On the first hill: a grand Senate House with a colonnaded portico and bronze dome beside a vast forum of white marble, statues, and triumphal columns. On the second: a fortified imperial palace-citadel with thick walls and towers. On the third: a colossal arena like a giant amphitheater, partly ruined on one side. A broad triumphal road lined with seven giant god-statues runs through a monumental arch into the city. Aqueducts stride across the landscape into the city on tall arches. Dense districts of red-tiled roofs, insulae, temples with columned fronts, baths, and gardens fill the valleys between the hills. Massive defensive walls with square towers encircle everything. A busy harbor with galleys and warehouses at the river mouth. Outside the walls: villas, vineyards, olive groves, cypress-lined roads, farming estates, and smaller towns along straight paved roads converging on the capital. Warm, golden, powerful, and proud.

## 3. Goldspire Rock (Greek, fortress carved into a colossal sea cliff)

> Goldspire Rock, a colossal golden-brown sea cliff rising sheer from the ocean, and the fortress of a wealthy Greek-inspired merchant dynasty carved directly INTO the rock face itself. The castle is the rock: tiers of halls, balconies, colonnades, and white marble temple-fronts cut into the cliff, towers emerging from the stone, gold-roofed spires at the summit, great carved gateways, stairways spiraling up the cliff face, and mine entrances glowing with warm light where gold is dug from deep inside. A gigantic carved statue of a robed figure holding a golden torch is cut into the rock beside the main gate. At the FOOT of the cliff, a dense white Greek city spreads along the shore: white stone houses with terracotta roofs, temples with columns, an agora, theaters cut into the slopes, and a large protected harbor crowded with triremes and merchant ships behind a stone breakwater with lighthouse towers. Roads and terraced olive groves climb the hills beside the cliff; small villages, vineyards, and mining camps dot the surrounding coast. The image should make clear that the rock is the castle, and the city lives at its feet. Rich, gleaming, and mighty.

## 4. Frosthold (Medieval north, grand Gothic fortress)

> Frosthold, the seat of a devout northern medieval dynasty, a grand Gothic fortress-city in a snowy forested valley. Massive grey stone double walls with round towers and fortified gatehouses enclose a huge castle: a towering keep with steep slate roofs, soaring Gothic cathedral spires with pointed arches, flying buttresses, and stained-glass windows glowing warm against the snow. Steam rises from natural hot springs inside the walls, feeding steaming pools and glass-roofed winter gardens. In a sacred grove within the fortress stands a colossal ancient tree with pale white bark and blood-red leaves, the only red in a white-and-grey world. Heavy banners hang from the walls. Outside the walls, a sprawling winter town of timber-framed houses with snow-covered roofs and smoking chimneys, stables, smithies, and a market, then farmsteads, frozen fields, pine forests, and roads cutting through the snow toward smaller villages and watchtowers. Light snow on the ground, crisp and clear sky. Stern, holy, and unbreakable.

## 5. The Greywall and the Wardens' Gate (colossal wall across the north)

> The Greywall, a colossal fortified wall stretching from horizon to horizon across a frozen northern land, hundreds of feet tall, built of enormous grey stone blocks glazed with ice and snow. The wall is so long it disappears into the distance in both directions, with watchtowers and signal-fire beacons at intervals along its top. At the center of the image sits the Wardens' Gate: a massive fortress built against the wall's base, with stone barracks, a keep, courtyards, training yards, a switchback stairway and a hanging lift cage climbing the wall face, and a great iron-bound gate through the wall. Tiny figures patrol the battlements. On the south side: the Wardens' garrison town, supply roads, wagon trains bringing men and supplies, and bleak farmland. On the north side, beyond the wall: a hostile frozen wasteland of tundra, glaciers, and dark pine, with distant orc war-camp smoke rising and crude totems on the horizon. The scale should feel staggering and grim, the last defense of civilization.

## 6. Skyreach (fortress city on a mountain peak, brutal and epic)

> Skyreach, a brutal and epic fortress-city built on and around a needle-like mountain peak that rises from a ring of high, jagged mountains above a fertile valley. The fortress is massive and martial: tiers of heavy stone walls and bastions wrapped around the peak, great buttresses and defensive galleries anchored into the rock, thick towers with conical roofs, giant iron chains and lift mechanisms hauling cargo up the cliffs, and a dominating citadel crowning the summit with tall towers and banners. A single stair road is carved into the mountain, winding up past fortified waystation forts at intervals. Waterfalls spill from the cliffs. Below, in the green valley at the mountain's foot: a walled lower town, farms, orchards, lakes, and roads leading to villages, with the entire valley protected by the surrounding mountain wall. It should feel impregnable, heroic, and dramatic.

## 7. Harrow Crossing (two great castles joined by a fortified bridge)

> Harrow Crossing, a grand and well-kept medieval fortress-crossing over a wide, powerful river. Two identical, magnificent castles face each other from opposite banks, each with high curtain walls, round towers, a great keep, and clean, well-maintained stone. They are joined by a long, monumental fortified bridge of many arches, with a tall central tower and gatehouse at its midpoint, battlements along its length, and toll houses. River barges and boats queue at water gates beneath the bridge; a busy river harbor serves each castle. On both banks, prosperous market towns with timber-framed houses, warehouses, inns, and a bustling market, then fertile river farmland, mills with water wheels, and roads converging on the crossing from all directions. Banners fly from every tower. It should feel grand, prosperous, and strategically vital, the gateway of the land.

## 8. Highbloom (Roman garden city; the player's choices shape it)

> Highbloom, an elegant Roman-inspired city crowning a gentle hill in rich farmland on a river, shown at its grandest. Outside the walls, an abundant patchwork of vineyards, wheat fields, olive groves, orchards, farm estates, and small villages along the river, with river boats carrying produce. ## 9. Oldstone Citadel (scholar-fortress port)

> Oldstone Citadel, a fortified scholar-city at a river mouth on a southwestern coast, where learning and military strength are blended. At its center rises a colossal lighthouse tower of stacked stone tiers, far taller than anything around it, with a great fire burning at the top. Around the tower: library domes, observatories with bronze telescopes and astronomical instruments, scholars' halls and cloisters, all set inside heavy military fortifications: thick angled walls, bastions with ballistae and catapults, a fortified harbor with a chain boom across its mouth, barracks, and a war-galley dock. Dense districts of stone houses and tiled roofs fill the rest of the city. Outside: a fortified harbor town, coastal villages, farmland, and roads along the coast and river. It should feel learned, ancient, and well defended, a place where scholars and soldiers serve the same walls.

## 10. Tempest Keep (one colossal drum tower on a storm cliff)

> Tempest Keep, a colossal fortress dominated by one enormous round drum tower of dark weathered stone, impossibly wide and tall, standing on a sheer cliff above a wild sea. The tower is ringed by a massive curtain wall with its own towers and a fortified gatehouse, and its stone is scarred, ancient, and immovable. Waves crash against the cliff base far below. A fortified town nestles beneath the cliffs around a small storm-sheltered harbor with a heavy sea wall, fishing boats, and war galleys. Roads wind inland through rain-forest and rocky headlands to smaller villages. A storm is passing out at sea: dark clouds and a distant lightning flash offshore, but the keep itself is in clear light so its shape reads clearly. It should feel unbreakable, brooding, and epic, a fortress that has never fallen.

## 11. The Skulkmire (ratmen city rising from a drowned swamp)

> The Skulkmire, a sprawling ratmen city rising from a drowned swamp of ancient ruins in a dark rain-forest. The ratmen are not elegant architects, but this is an impressive, massive, and unique city: towers and halls built from old timber, scavenged stone, iron plates, nails, and rope, piled high and crooked over the half-sunken ruins of an older civilization's temples. Tall crooked towers, a central great bell tower, walkways and rope bridges strung between structures, cranes and scaffolds, pipes and chimneys belching thick green-glowing smoke, sickly green light glowing from tunnels and windows. Wooden docks and stilt platforms over black water, rickety boats, and tunnel entrances everywhere. Around the city: swamp farms of fungus and reeds, scavenger camps, smaller stilt villages, and hidden paths through the drowned forest. Grimy, industrious, sinister, and surprisingly vast.

## 12. Emberdeep (dwarf capital carved into a red mountain)

> Emberdeep, the capital hold of the dwarves, carved into the face of a towering red-rock mountain. Dominating the scene is a colossal gate flanked by two gigantic carved statues of armored dwarf kings, standing, each gripping a great rune-carved warhammer, their stone beards flowing to their feet. Above and around the gate, the mountain face is carved into tiers of fortified halls, balconies, stair-roads, and stone bridges spanning ravines, with heavy angular dwarven architecture, brass and bronze fittings, and glowing runes. Forge vents and chimneys pour orange fire-glow and smoke from the mountainside, with rivers of molten metal glowing in channels near the foundries. A massive fortified road climbs to the gate through outer bastions and watchtowers. At the mountain's foot: a stone-built outer town, mine entrances, ore carts on tracks, quarries, smelters, and roads to smaller mining holds in the surrounding peaks. Mighty, ancient, industrious, and unbreakable.

## 13. Brinecrag (dark elf reaver fortress on sea stacks)

> Brinecrag, an epic and brutal dark elf fortress built across a cluster of towering black sea stacks rising from a grey, violent sea. Each stack is crowned with a tall, sinister, spiked tower of dark stone and black iron with sharp angular spires, cruel ornamentation, and violet lamplight glowing from narrow windows. The towers are linked by chain bridges and narrow stone walkways high above crashing waves. Below, carved into the rock and built on dark stone quays: slave pens, prison cages hanging over the water, shipyards, and a harbor packed with sleek, menacing raider ships with black sails and bladed prows. Hooks, chains, and grim trophies hang from the walls. On the nearby grey island: a dark-stone town of sharp roofs, slave markets, watchtowers, and rocky paths. Elegant in a cruel way, but above all epic, menacing, and brutal, a pirate fortress feared across the western sea.

## Design notes to send CC later (from your answers)

- **Settlement levels:** TW:WH3 uses up to 5 levels for major settlements; consider moving from 3 to 5 levels (likely post-V1).
- **Specialization paths:** every settlement's look follows a committed player choice: **military, farming, mining, lumber, or market**. Each path has a fixed, designed appearance per culture (no on-the-fly generation).
- **Goldspire redesign:** the castle should be carved into the rock, with the city at its foot (current build is a city sitting on top of a rock).
- **Every race builds impressive cities**, with architecture reflecting how the race operates (elves clean and refined, ratmen timber and scrap but still massive).
- **Alive at scale:** use tiny, simple details (specks of people, smoke, carts, birds as brush strokes) to make settlements feel alive.
