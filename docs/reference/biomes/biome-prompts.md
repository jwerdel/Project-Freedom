# Culture Biome Board Prompts (Gemini) v2

**How to use:** paste the **Style Header**, then one culture prompt, as a single message. Generate 2-4 variations and keep the best. Save to `docs/reference/biomes/<culture>.jpg` (e.g. `orcs.jpg`). Reference only, never shipped.

**Consistency:** use the same Style Header every time. For best results, attach one of your finished landmark images and add: "Match the art style, lighting, and camera angle of the attached image exactly."

**Optional winter variant:** after you have the summer image, generate a second version by attaching it and adding the **Winter Suffix** (bottom of this file). Save as `<culture>_winter.jpg`. Useful for the long-seasons feature, but not required now.

**What these boards show:** the race's baseline land and architecture. Individual factions within a race (each with its own crest, colors, and place on the good-to-evil spectrum) come later with heraldry, so keep these boards free of specific faction banners.

---

## Style Header (paste before every prompt)

> Epic high-fantasy strategy game campaign map illustration. Painterly, richly detailed digital painting with vivid, saturated colors, bold contrast, and heroic exaggerated scale, in the colorful, larger-than-life style of modern fantasy grand-strategy campaign maps: bright and readable even for grim peoples, with menace shown through shapes and details rather than murky darkness. Camera: high three-quarter aerial view, about 50 degrees down, the way a player sees land on a campaign map. The image shows a wide stretch of one culture's homeland in summer with a medium-sized settlement of that culture near the center, so both the LAND and the ARCHITECTURE read clearly. Show clearly: ground colors and textures, ground cover, tree types, water, roads, fields or other land use, weather, and the shapes, materials, roofs, and walls of the buildings. Lighting: clear late-morning daylight from the upper left, soft shadows, minimal fog, so every shape reads clearly (this is a reference for building 3D terrain and buildings). The land feels alive through tiny, simple details: specks of people on roads, carts, chimney smoke, small boats, birds suggested by a couple of brush strokes. The settlement is impressive and well built for its culture, never shabby. Neutral, unmarked banners only. Original design only: no text, no labels, no UI, no logos, no heraldic crests, no real-world religious symbols, no symbols from existing games or franchises. Landscape 16:9.

## 1. Medieval humans (the North)

> The homeland of a devout northern medieval kingdom in summer. Cool temperate land, mostly green: lush meadows, dark pine and oak forests, rolling hills, cold clear lakes and rivers, grey stone outcrops; snow only on the highest distant peaks. A medium-sized fortified town of grey stone and dark timber: a stone castle with round towers and a keep, a Gothic church with a tall pointed spire and stained glass, timber-framed houses with steep slate and thatch roofs, stone curtain walls with gatehouses, a market square, smithies, mills with water wheels. Around it: patchwork fields with hedgerows and dry-stone walls, sheep pastures, farmsteads, a winding dirt road, a small stone bridge, watchtowers on the hills. Palette: deep greens, cool greys, slate blue, warm timber browns. Stern, devout, hardy.

## 2. Roman humans (the southern heartland)

> The homeland of a fantasy Roman civilization in summer. Warm, lush, and fertile, deliberately different from a dry Greek coast: rolling green-gold farmland, broad river valleys, tall dark cypress avenues, umbrella pines, olive groves, neat vineyards, wheat fields, reddish-brown earth. A medium-sized Roman town of cream and warm sandstone with bold red terracotta roofs: a massive columned temple on a raised podium, a forum with colonnades and bronze statues, a domed bathhouse, a small amphitheater of arches, square-towered defensive walls with a triumphal arched gate, a legion fortress with neat rows of tents and a palisade nearby. Straight paved roads lined with milestones lead to the town; an aqueduct on tall arches strides across the land; villas with courtyards and gardens dot the countryside. Palette: red terracotta, cream stone, bronze, deep green, gold. Orderly, prosperous, imperial, built on straight lines and arches.

## 3. Greek humans (the western coast)

> The homeland of fantasy Greek city-states on a sunlit coast in summer. Deliberately different from the green Roman heartland: dry, rocky, sun-bleached hills in tan, ochre, and gold, silver-green olive groves, scrub and wild herbs, terraced slopes, white sandy coves, a brilliant turquoise sea with islands. A medium-sized Greek city of brilliant white marble with blue-painted details and pale roofs climbing a seaside hill: a temple with slender white columns on the hilltop acropolis, a theater carved into the slope, an agora with stoas, cube-like white houses with courtyards, city walls running down to the sea, a harbor packed with triremes and merchant ships behind a stone breakwater with a lighthouse. Around it: olive presses, vineyards, goat herds on rocky paths, small white villages on headlands and islands. Palette: white, sky blue, turquoise, ochre, olive green. Bright, elegant, seafaring, built on columns and open plazas.

## 4. Tomb-King desert humans (the Sahren)

Palette: gold, sand, terracotta red, lapis blue, turquoise, palm green. Ancient, magical, majestic, and life-giving.

## 5. Dwarves (the Red Peaks)

## 6. Orcs (beyond the Greywall)

Savage, energetic, intimidating.

## 9. Beastmen (the Horned Sea)

> The homeland of the beastmen herds in an endless wild grassland in summer. ## 10. Ratmen (the Skulkmire swamps)

> The homeland of the ratmen in a rain-forest swamp in summer. A medium-sized ratmen city that is crooked and scrap-built but massive and impressive: tall leaning towers of old timber, scavenged stone, iron plates, nails, and rope, built over sunken ancient ruins; walkways and rope bridges between structures; pipes and chimneys belching smoke with a green tint; a great bell tower; stilt platforms and docks; tunnel entrances glowing faintly green. Grimy, industrious, sinister, surprisingly vast.

## 11. Lizardmen (the Sothmire jungle)

Their architecture is breathtaking, the most monumental of any people: colossal stepped pyramids of precisely cut golden stone with steep stairways and glowing star-altars at their peaks, enormous serpent and reptile statues, great causeways and plazas, carved stone gates, intricate geometric reliefs inlaid with jade and gold, golden light pouring from temple chambers. Palette: jungle green, golden stone, jade, turquoise water, warm gold light. Ancient, mysterious, orderly, awe-inspiring.

## Design notes to send CC later (from your answers)

- **Faction identity within races:** every faction has its own crest, colors, and feel, and sits somewhere on a good-to-evil spectrum (some Roman houses noble, others cruel). Same for every race.
- **Game of Thrones seasons:** long, irregular seasons (summers that can last ~7 years, winters of 1-2 years), shown on the map. Effects on gameplay to be designed.
- **Visual style:** vivid, colorful, Total War style, even for grim races.
- **Extreme conversion is allowed:** orcs can turn a desert to snow, lizardmen can turn tundra to jungle.
- **Mixed populations blend:** a region with a mixed population shows a blend of both cultures' land and architecture.
- **Desert magic is benevolent and visible** (enchanted crops, glowing springs); **beastmen architecture grows from the wild**; **ratmen pollution is mild**.
