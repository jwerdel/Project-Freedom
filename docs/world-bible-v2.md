# Project Freedom: World Bible v2

**Status:** Draft v2 for owner review. Supersedes `docs/world.md` (v1) once approved.
**Scope:** Lore, geography, peoples, faiths, factions, and landmarks. Mechanics live in `docs/game-design.md` and `constitution.md`; where this file describes how something works in play, it is flavor that the design doc makes concrete.
**Tone:** Game of Thrones politics meets Warhammer brutality. Leans dark. Betrayal, executions, holy wars, and monstrous enemies are all on the table.
**Names:** Every name here is a placeholder the owner can change. In play, the player can rename every settlement and character.

## 1. The world at a glance

The world is **Varos**. It has three landmasses plus a blighted far east.

| Land | Inspired by | Who lives there | In V1? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aldryn** (west) | Westeros | Orcs (far north), Medieval humans (north), the Throne City (center), Roman humans (south and east), Greek humans (west coast), dark elves (western isles), dwarves (southern mountains), Tomb-King humans (southern desert), a ratmen enclave (southeastern swamps) | Yes |
| **Ossara** (east, across the Narrow Sea) | Essos | Elves (western coast), beastmen (inland grass sea) | Yes |
| **The Ashlands** (far east of Ossara) | Shadow Lands, Asshai | The Hollow Dynasty (Necron-like), the Unbound Hosts (Chaos-like) | **No, post-V1** |
| **Sothmire** (far south, across the high seas) | Sothoryos | Ratmen hive (north coast), lizardmen (jungle heart) | Yes |

**Terrain:** everything should read like Westeros. Green temperate lowlands, rolling hills, wide rivers, rugged coasts, a snowbound north, a red southern desert, and mountains that matter. Ossara shifts from lush coast to endless grassland. Sothmire is fog, jungle, and black rock.

**Climate matters:** every people thrives in its homeland and struggles elsewhere. A dwarf hold in the mountains yields far more than the same hold in a jungle.

## 2. Deep history (what everyone half-remembers)

### The God-Emperor
Two thousand years ago, a single human warlord united the scattered tribes of men against the orcs, beastmen, and darker things of the age. He was called the **God-Emperor**. He did not die. At the height of his power he sat upon the **Undying Throne** in the city he founded at the heart of Aldryn, and he has not risen since. He is alive in some sense nobody can explain: he does not speak, does not age, does not move. His church says he holds the world together by will alone.

He is a **figurehead in play**, never a playable hero or a character who acts. His presence is felt through his church, his city, and the faith of those who believe.

### The Seven
Before he took the throne, the God-Emperor raised seven companions to carry his word to the far corners of the land. These **Seven** founded temples, laws, and cities across the south and west. Over the centuries, the peoples of the warm south and the western coast came to worship the Seven themselves as gods, and the Emperor faded in their telling into a distant first father. Today:
- **The northern Medieval peoples** worship the God-Emperor directly. To them, the Seven were holy servants, not gods.
- **The Roman and Greek peoples** worship the **Radiant Seven** as a pantheon. They still honor the Emperor, and still revere his city, but as the ancient root of their faith rather than its living center.

Both faiths share one origin. That makes them uneasy cousins: capable of brotherhood, and capable of the bitterest schisms.

### The Throne City
The God-Emperor's city, **Caeloth**, stands at the heart of Aldryn where the great rivers meet and the marshes form natural moats (Moat Cailin was the inspiration, reimagined as a vast metropolis rather than a ruin). It is the richest city in the world, the seat of the **Throne Church**, a pilgrimage site for every human culture, and a trading crossroads for all of Aldryn. No human faction is meant to take it. Everyone schemes for influence there instead.

### The Sundering of the Elves
Long ago the elves of the Ossaran coast fought a civil war over the succession of their seers. The losers were cast out across the sea. They settled the grey western isles off Aldryn's coast and became the **dark elves**: reavers, slavers, and pirates who hate their kin above all else.

### The Fall of the Hollow Dynasty
In the far east, an ancient desert people much like the Tomb Kings of the southern desert once ruled from black pyramids. Their priest-kings feared death so much that they bargained with the powers beyond the Rift for eternal life. They got it: their flesh became metal, their souls burned away, and they became the **Hollow Dynasty**, unliving and unfeeling. They are a living warning to the desert peoples of Aldryn about where that road leads. *(Post-V1 content; the lore is established now.)*

### The Greywall
After the God-Emperor's wars, the northern kingdoms raised the **Greywall**, a colossal fortified wall of ice-clad stone across the far north, to keep the orc tribes out. It is held by a sworn order, the **Wardens**. If it is neglected, it will fall.

### The Long Stillness (why there are no starting wars)
For the last generation, an edict proclaimed from the Undying Throne, the **Stillness**, forbade the human realms from making war on one another. Its proclamation has expired at campaign start. Nobody is at war yet. Nobody is allied yet. But everyone remembers every slight from before the Stillness, and every realm is arming.

## 3. Order, Destruction, and the in-between

| Bloc | Peoples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Order** | Humans (Medieval, Roman, Greek), elves, dwarves | Find it easy to cooperate. Not always friendly, but they share a sense that the world must be held together. |
| **Neutral, leaning Order** | Lizardmen | Ancient and aloof. Locked in a blood feud with the ratmen colonizing their coast. |
| **Neutral (true swing)** | Tomb-King desert humans | Human, but alien in their death-magic. Can side with Order or Destruction with equal ease. Everyone courts them. |
| **Destruction** | Orcs, beastmen, ratmen, dark elves | Cooperate with each other more easily than with Order. |
| **Beyond** | Hollow Dynasty, Unbound Hosts | World-ending threats. Post-V1. |

Crossing the line is possible but costly: an Order realm that allies with orcs pays for it in reputation with its own kind, and gains respect from the other side. A human who schemes against other humans earns quiet admiration from orcs and ratmen.

## 4. Faiths

### The Throne Church (God-Emperor)
- **Followers:** Medieval humans.
- **Seat:** Caeloth, the Throne City.
- **Hierarchy:** the **Voice of the Throne** (the high priest, elected), the **Exarchs** (senior prelates who vote in elections, drawn from pious priests of every faithful realm), and ordinary priests.
- **Powers:** missions to faithful realms, **Holy Wars** against heretics, Destruction peoples, or the anathematized; **Anathema** (excommunication); the **Inquisition**.
- **Character:** stern, hierarchical, apocalyptic. Believes the Emperor's will is all that holds back the dark.

### The Radiant Seven
- **Followers:** Roman and Greek humans.
- **The Seven** (names placeholder):

| God | Domain | Favored by |
|---|---|---|
| **Aurel** | Light, law, kingship | Rulers and judges |
| **Vessa** | Hearth, harvest, family | Farmers, mothers, villages |
| **Kael** | War, courage, triumph | Soldiers and generals |
| **Merovan** | Roads, trade, travelers | Merchants |
| **Ilith** | Wisdom, prophecy | Oracles and scholars |
| **Thalor** | Sea, storms, voyages | Sailors and coastal cities |
| **Morra** | Death, memory, the ancestors | Mourners and historians |

- **Character:** a pantheon, temple-based, worldly. Romans emphasize a **state cult** (the Seven as patrons of the Republic, omens read before war). Greeks emphasize **oracles** and city patron gods (each city devoted to one of the Seven).
- **Relationship to the Throne Church:** uneasy cousins. Both revere Caeloth. Theological disputes over "who truly speaks for the Emperor's legacy" are a lever for schemes.

### Other faiths
| Faith | People | Political flavor |
|---|---|---|
| **The Eternal Sun** | Tomb-King desert humans | God-kings, mortuary priesthood, the dead serve the living. |
| **The Forge Fathers** | Dwarves | Ancestor worship; the **Book of Grudges** records every wrong that must be avenged. |
| **The Starweave** | Elves | Fate written in the stars; seers issue prophecies. |
| **The Severed Star** | Dark elves | A twisted Starweave: fate is something you seize by blood. |
| **Blood and Iron** | Orcs | Strength is holy. The strongest chieftain speaks for the gods. |
| **The Horned Dark** | Beastmen | Herdstones, primal hunger, ruin as worship. |
| **The Great Gnawing** | Ratmen | A hidden rat god who promises the world to the patient and treacherous. |
| **The Old Plan** | Lizardmen | Star-architects left a design. The lizardmen serve it. |
| **The Silence** | Hollow Dynasty | No faith. Protocol. *(Post-V1.)* |
| **The Unbound** | Unbound Hosts | Four hungry powers beyond the Rift. Mortals can be tempted. *(Post-V1.)* |

## 5. Peoples and cultures

### 5.1 Humans: Medieval (the North)
- **Look and feel:** castles, mail and plate, knights, cold forests, longhalls, heraldry, cathedrals.
- **Faith:** the Throne Church.
- **Politics:** feudal. Lords hold land from their liege in exchange for service. Knightly orders. The Church is a power in every court.
- **Strengths:** heavy cavalry and knights, sturdy castles, holy warriors, strong ties to Caeloth.
- **Mythic monsters (examples):** war griffons bred in the high north, the Throne Church's armored holy war-beasts, trained winter bears.
- **Naming:** Anglo-Norman and Germanic (Edric, Aldous, Rowena, Berthold; Houses Varn, Dunmoor, Kells).

### 5.2 Humans: Roman (the southern heartland)
- **Look and feel:** marble, roads, legions, aqueducts, laurels, forums, triumphal arches.
- **Faith:** the Radiant Seven as state cult.
- **Politics:** a Republic of great families. Power flows through the **Senate**, offices, patronage, and marriages. The families must be united under one house.
- **Strengths:** disciplined legions, engineering, roads, administration, client states.
- **Mythic monsters (examples):** war elephants from southern trade, chained arena beasts, sacred eagles, siege-titans of bronze.
- **Naming:** Latinate (Lucan, Cassia, Severin, Octavian; families Varrenus, Corvinus, Tullan, Aemerius).

### 5.3 Humans: Greek (the western coast)
- **Look and feel:** white cities on sea cliffs, columns, triremes, hoplites, theaters, oracle shrines.
- **Faith:** the Radiant Seven; each city has a patron god; oracles matter.
- **Politics:** fiercely independent **city-states**. Leagues, tyrants, democracies, colonies. Unite them by persuasion, leagues, trade, or the spear.
- **Strengths:** phalanxes, navies, trade, philosophy, colonies.
- **Mythic monsters (examples):** griffons, centaurs, bronze automatons, sea serpents, the cyclops of the cliff-caves.
- **Naming:** Hellenic (Kallias, Theron, Iolanthe, Demetria; families Aurekos, Philandros, Lykourgides).

### 5.4 Humans: Tomb-King desert peoples (the Sahren)
- **Look and feel:** red dunes, oasis rivers, step pyramids, obelisks, embalmed kings, gold and lapis.
- **Faith:** the Eternal Sun.
- **Politics:** living god-kings guided by a **mortuary priesthood** of death-mages who can wake the honored dead to fight. Neutral swing people: they can court Order or Destruction.
- **Strengths:** powerful magic, risen ancestor armies, monuments.
- **Mythic monsters (examples):** bone-giants, sphinx-statues that walk, scorpion constructs, carrion birds.
- **Warning in their own lore:** the Hollow Dynasty of the far east was once a people like them.
- **AI-only in V1.**

### 5.5 Dwarves (the Red Peaks)
- **Home:** holds carved into the Red Peaks between the green south and the desert.
- **Faith:** the Forge Fathers.
- **Politics:** clans under a High King. Honor, memory, oaths. The **Book of Grudges** records every wrong; unavenged grudges shame the king.
- **Strengths:** unbreakable infantry, engineering, underground travel between holds.
- **AI-only in V1.**

### 5.6 Orcs (beyond the Greywall)
- **Home:** the frozen waste of tundra, glacier, and pine beyond the Greywall.
- **Faith:** Blood and Iron.
- **Politics:** strength is legitimacy. Chieftains rule by challenge. A great chieftain can call a vast horde.
- **Relations:** most orc tribes hate men forever. A small minority (the Redhand) seek friendship and are despised by other orcs for it.
- **AI-only in V1.**

### 5.7 Elves (the Ossaran coast)
- **Home:** a chain of ancient city-states along Ossara's western coast, facing Aldryn across the Narrow Sea.
- **Faith:** the Starweave. Seers matter enormously.
- **Politics:** old, proud, contemptuous of younger races, rich in trade and magic. Hate the dark elves who were cast out.
- **AI-only in V1. First race planned to become playable after V1.**

### 5.8 Dark elves (the Western Isles)
- **Home:** the grey, storm-lashed isles off Aldryn's western coast (the Iron Islands, reimagined).
- **Faith:** the Severed Star.
- **Politics:** reaver captains, slaver markets, a cruel aristocracy. Raid trade routes and coasts. Their great **Black Ark** is a floating fortress-city that moves.
- **AI-only in V1.**

### 5.9 Beastmen (the Horned Sea)
- **Home:** the endless grassland interior of Ossara.
- **Faith:** the Horned Dark.
- **Politics:** herds, dominance, ruin. No cities: they roam and raze.
- **AI-only in V1.**

### 5.10 Ratmen (the Skulkmire and Gnawholm)
- **Homes:** the Skulkmire swamps of the southeastern Aldryn rainwood (a hidden scout-hive), and the great hive of **Gnawholm** on Sothmire's north coast.
- **Faith:** the Great Gnawing.
- **Politics:** treachery is culture. Clans backstab constantly; the most treacherous rise. They tunnel underground between their warrens.
- **AI-only in V1.**

### 5.11 Lizardmen (the Sothmire jungle)
- **Home:** temple-cities in the jungle heart of Sothmire.
- **Faith:** the Old Plan.
- **Politics:** ancient, orderly, aloof. Servants of a design older than men. Blood feud with the ratmen colonizing their coast.
- **AI-only in V1.**

### 5.12 Hollow Dynasty and Unbound Hosts *(post-V1)*
- **Hollow Dynasty** (Necron-like): fallen Tomb-King-like priest-kings turned to metal by a Chaos bargain. Black obsidian pyramids with green-burning seams, waking slowly. Formerly named "Silent Dynasty of Nehket" in v1; renamed to avoid confusion with the desert peoples.
- **Unbound Hosts** (Chaos-like): warbands pouring from the **Rift**, growing by crushing minor factions, then invading west. Mortals of any race can be tempted to serve them.

## 6. Geography, region by region

### Aldryn

**The Frostwaste (far north).** Beyond the Greywall: tundra, glacier, pine, frozen fjords. Orc tribes. The orc capital **Ghurmak** is a war-camp inside the ribcage of a gigantic frozen beast.

**The Greywall.** The great wall across the north, held by the **Wardens**, a sworn order recruiting exiles, criminals, and second sons. If the realms of men stop sending men and gold, it weakens, and the orcs pour through.

**The North (Medieval).** Vast cold forests, moors, lakes, longhalls and stone castles. Home of the playable **House Varn of Frosthold** and its vassal and rival houses.

**The Vale (Medieval).** High mountains around a fertile valley. **Skyreach**, nearly impregnable, sits on a peak. Medieval culture.

**The Middle Marches (around Caeloth).** Rich, flat river country (the Riverlands, reimagined), split among many small houses of mixed culture. Everyone's battlefield, everyone's pilgrimage road, and the prime early consolidation zone for whichever realm moves first.

**Caeloth, the Throne City.** At the heart of Aldryn where the great rivers meet. Marsh-moats, colossal walls, cathedral-spires, markets from every land. Untouchable seat of the Throne Church.

**The Crownlands, the Reach, and the Stormlands (Roman).** The warm southern heartland: fields, orchards, vineyards, roads, rain-forests in the east, storm coasts. The great Roman families hold it, with **Crownhaven** (old imperial capital) as their seat of the Senate. Home of the playable **House Varrenus**.

**The Skulkmire.** Drowned ruins in the eastern rainwood swamps. A hidden ratmen warren.

**The Westerlands Coast (Greek).** Sea cliffs, gold mines, harbor cities, olive hills. Fiercely independent city-states. Home of the playable **Aurekid house of Goldspire Rock**.

**The Western Isles (dark elves).** Grey sea stacks, storm coasts, slaver ports. Dark elf reavers. The fortress **Brinecrag** and the roaming **Black Ark**.

**The Red Peaks (dwarves).** The mountain wall between the green south and the desert. Dwarf holds: **Emberdeep**, **Sunforge**, **Karvak**.

**The Sahren (Tomb-King desert).** Red dunes, oasis rivers, canyon cities, pyramids. The Dominions of **Qaseth** and **Mer-Khet**.

### Ossara

**The Elven Coast.** Elven city-states: **Aelthas** (lagoon city behind a titan statue, home of the Moonvault bank), **Lysar** (white spires and seers), **Therin** (merchant princes), **Vael'Tor** (old imperial elves who still think they rule the world).

**The Horned Sea.** An ocean of grass. Beastmen herds: **Bloodhorn**, **Gorehoof**, **Ashmane**. Their sacred site **Hornstone** is a ring of monoliths around a blood-stained herdstone.

**The Ashlands** *(post-V1).* Ash plains, dead cities, the **Rift**.

### Sothmire

**The Gnawing Coast.** The great ratmen hive of **Gnawholm**, a mountain of crooked towers belching green smoke.

**The Jungle Heart.** The lizardmen temple-city of **Xal-Tzotl** and the younger **Serpent Coast** spawnings.

## 7. Factions

Faction counts are deliberately crowded. Every major faction is surrounded by small minor factions it can absorb quickly (war, diplomacy, marriage, or the Liberator's Call).

### 7.1 Playable in V1 (one per human culture)

#### House Varn of Frosthold (Medieval, Throne Church)
- **Founder (legendary, immortal):** **Edric Varn, the Winter Shield**. Grim, devout, honorable to a fault. Traits: Devout, Stalwart, Mistrustful of Southerners.
- **House tradition:** holy warriors and castles. Bonuses to Throne Church influence, fortress defense, and knightly units.
- **Start:** Frosthold and its valley (one or two settlements). Small and humble.
- **Neighbors:** minor northern houses (Dunmoor, Kells of the Barrowlands), the Wardens of the Greywall to the north, the Vale to the southeast.
- **Liberator's Call:** **House Dunmoor**, whose lord is a drunkard and tyrant; his people will send an envoy early.
- **Story pressure:** the Greywall needs men; the orcs are restless.

#### House Varrenus (Roman, Radiant Seven)
- **Founder (legendary, immortal):** **Lucan Varrenus, the Silver Tongue**. A senator and patrician who believes Rome can be reborn under one house. Traits: Orator, Schemer, Patron of the Seven.
- **House tradition:** the Senate and patronage. Bonuses to influence over other families, marriage alliances, and legion discipline.
- **Start:** one estate-city near Crownhaven. Small and humble.
- **Neighbors:** rival great families (Corvinus, Tullan, Aemerius) as minor factions, the Senate at Crownhaven, the Stormlands' martial houses to the east.
- **Liberator's Call:** **the Tullan estates**, whose heir murdered his father and is hated by his clients.
- **Story pressure:** unite the families under one house before another does.

#### The Aurekids of Goldspire Rock (Greek, Radiant Seven)
- **Founder (legendary, immortal):** **Kallias Aurekos, the Gold-Handed**. Merchant-tyrant of a single rich city who dreams of a league of the whole coast. Traits: Wealthy, Shrewd, Patron of Merovan.
- **House tradition:** trade and the sea. Bonuses to trade income, navies, and mines.
- **Start:** Goldspire Rock (the colossal sea cliff with its gold mines). Small and humble but rich.
- **Neighbors:** free cities up and down the coast (Silverfall, Theros, Kyme), dark elf reavers offshore.
- **Liberator's Call:** **the city of Silverfall**, ruled by a tyrant who seized power by murdering its council.
- **Story pressure:** unite the free cities into one league before the dark elves pick them apart.

## 8. Landmarks

Each landmark has a unique campaign-map model with three growth stages (stage 1 recognizable by silhouette). Culture changes from v1 are noted.

### Aldryn
1. **Caeloth, the Throne City** *(new; holy city)*: a vast walled metropolis ringed by marsh-moats where great rivers meet. Signature: the cathedral of the **Undying Throne**, its dome visible from leagues away; bridges and market-islands; pilgrim roads converging. Stages: grows with Throne Church wealth.
2. **Crownhaven** *(now Roman)*: the old imperial capital on three hills. Signature: the **Senate House**, a forum, and the triumphal **Road of the Seven** lined with god-statues; a ruined arena (the Wyrm Pit).
3. **Goldspire Rock** *(now Greek; already built as a prototype landmark)*: a colossal sea cliff with glowing mine mouths, gold-roofed towers, terraced halls, and harbor. Stage art can gain Greek columns and temples.
4. **Frosthold** *(Medieval)*: grey double walls, hot springs steaming inside, the pale-barked **Hearthtree** in the godswood (kept as an old tradition the Throne Church tolerates).
5. **The Greywall and Wardens' Gate**: the colossal wall and the fortress at its base with a switchback stair and lift cage.
6. **Ghurmak** *(orcs)*: a war-camp inside the ribcage of a frozen giant beast.
7. **Skyreach** *(Medieval)*: white towers on a peak, one stair road, a basket lift.
8. **Harrow Crossing** *(Middle Marches)*: two identical castles joined by a fortified bridge.
9. **Highbloom** *(Roman)*: concentric rings of white walls with gardens and orchards.
10. **Oldstone Citadel** *(Roman)*: the scholar-tower lighthouse with library domes.
11. **Tempest Keep** *(Roman, House Durran)*: one enormous drum tower on a storm cliff.
12. **The Skulkmire** *(ratmen)*: drowned ruins with green-glowing chimneys poking out of the swamp.
13. **Emberdeep** *(dwarves)*: a gate carved as two colossal dwarf kings, forge vents glowing in the mountain face. Inside: the Hall of the **Book of Grudges**.
14. **Qaseth** *(desert)*: a gold-capped step pyramid, obelisks, canal-palace.
15. **Brinecrag** *(dark elves; was Saltborn)*: towers on sea stacks linked by rope bridges; slave pens; black sails.
16. **The Black Ark *Vael Mor*** *(dark elves; new)*: a moving landmark. A floating fortress-city that sails the western sea as an army-like entity.

### Ossara
17. **Aelthas** *(elves)*: lagoon city behind a colossal elf warrior statue straddling the harbor; canals; the Moonvault.
18. **Lysar** *(elves)*: impossibly tall white spires linked by bridges; a star observatory.
19. **Hornstone** *(beastmen)*: monolith ring around a blood-stained herdstone; trophy avenue of stolen statues.

### Sothmire
20. **Gnawholm** *(ratmen)*: crooked towers and chimneys belching green smoke, a great bell at the summit.
21. **Xal-Tzotl** *(lizardmen)*: a massive stepped pyramid with a glowing star-altar.

### Post-V1
22. **Sethrakh** *(Hollow Dynasty; was Nehket)*: black obsidian pyramids with green seams.
23. **The Riven Gate** *(Unbound)*: a fortress around a tear in reality.

## 9. Story seeds (hooks for events, quests, and schemes)

- **The empty Voice:** the Throne Church's Voice is old. When he dies, the election will set every faithful court scheming.
- **The heretic question:** some Exarchs whisper that Seven-worshippers are heretics. A Holy War against Roman or Greek realms is not unthinkable.
- **The Redhand offer:** the Redhand orcs seek a human ally. Whoever accepts gains a fierce friend and the scorn of other men.
- **The Wardens' plea:** the Greywall is undermanned. Whoever answers gains Church favor; whoever ignores it may see orcs in their fields.
- **The Desert's choice:** the Dominions of Qaseth and Mer-Khet are being courted by both blocs. Their choice could tip a war.
- **The Grudge:** Emberdeep's Book holds a grudge against a human house from before the Stillness. Someone could remind the dwarves of it.
- **The Ark sails:** the Black Ark has been sighted off the Greek coast. Free cities that stand alone will burn.
- **The Gnawholm whisper:** the Skulkmire Brood takes orders from across the sea. If the lizardmen ever strike Gnawholm, the Skulkmire will panic.
- **The pyramid in the east:** a merchant returns from the Ashlands with a green-glowing shard. *(Post-V1 seed.)*

## 11. Changes from world bible v1

- Humans split into **Medieval (north, Throne Church)**, **Roman (south and east, Radiant Seven)**, **Greek (west coast, Radiant Seven)**, plus **Tomb-King desert peoples** (neutral, AI-only in V1).
- **The God-Emperor** added: founder of the Seven, figurehead on the Undying Throne in **Caeloth**, the new holy city at the heart of Aldryn.
- The Radiant Seven are now the Emperor's companions, worshipped as gods by Romans and Greeks.
- **Hearthfire** and **Drowned Mother** faiths retired (the Hearthtree survives as a northern tradition).
- **The Western Isles are now dark elves** (cast out by the elves). Brinecrag kept; the Saltborn Kings removed. The Black Ark added.
- The desert peoples became **Tomb-King-like death-mages** (Eternal Sun).
- **Order / Destruction / Neutral** blocs defined.
- The Necron-like faction renamed to **the Hollow Dynasty of Sethrakh** and given its origin: fallen Tomb-King-like priest-kings.
- The Regency Council of Crownhaven and the empty-crown premise replaced by **the Long Stillness** expiring.
- Playable V1 factions: **House Varn (Medieval)**, **House Varrenus (Roman)**, **the Aurekids of Goldspire (Greek)**.
- House Aurek (Westerlands) becomes the Greek Aurekids; House Verrin of Highbloom and House Lannet become Roman/Greek AI or minor factions during the map build.
